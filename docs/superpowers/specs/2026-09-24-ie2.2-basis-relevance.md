# IE2.2 — Basis vs Relevance invariants

Base: `5cba86df269b9abe867dc705f65ef069c72ed90c` (IE2.1 closed). Design only.

## 1. Audit — what the repository actually does today

Grounded in the code at this head, not in the earlier plan.

**Basis and relevance exist as vocabulary, and nothing checks completeness.** IE2.1 added
`SERVES`/`EXCLUDES`, a legality matrix, cross-plane resolution and a forward-only seam. It
validates that a relation is *permitted*; it never asks whether an object has *enough* of
them. No code anywhere walks a basis chain or a relevance path.

**The two planes have genuinely different lifecycle representations**, and no single
abstraction spans them:

| plane | liveness is | evidence of it |
|---|---|---|
| `state.objects` | `lifecycle is ACTIVE` **and** `authority ∉ {REJECTED, SUPERSEDED}` | `closure._is_current` |
| `state.semantic.claims` | the asserting `ASSERT_CLAIM` judgment is still active | `semantic_view.active_judgment_ids` |
| `state.semantic.evidence` | **no liveness concept at all** | `EvidenceItem` has no status |

`SemanticClaim` carries no `lifecycle` field. Claim liveness is a *judgment* property, and
the repository already computes it. IE2.2 reuses these three, and must not invent a fourth.

**Evidence supersession deliberately does not invalidate claims.** `domain/evidence.py`
states it outright: lineage "carries NO semantic authority: a claim citing a superseded
version is neither superseded nor flagged by it." Any rule making a claim ungrounded because
its evidence was superseded would contradict accepted law. IE2.2 does not introduce one.

**The certified Slice-1 Requirement is `PROPOSED`, not `CANONICAL`**, and carries only a
`DERIVED_FROM` relation — **no `SERVES` edge at all**. Both certified ledgers hold exactly
one canonical root `Intent`, and in both the basis claim is live with its evidence present.
This single fact decides the compatibility strategy (§14).

## 2. The central law: two lawful modes of grounding

The mistake IE2.2 must avoid is requiring every canonical object to reach empirical
evidence. That would make goals, preferences, exclusions and decisions impossible without
fabricated evidence — and fabricated evidence to satisfy an invariant is worse than no
invariant.

A normative object is lawfully grounded in **exactly one of two ways**:

```
EVIDENTIAL      the object asserts something about the world
                → basis chain terminates in a LIVE SemanticClaim

AUTHORITATIVE   the object IS a choice
                → authenticated human authorship + covering AuthorityRecord
```

Authoritative grounding is not a weaker form of evidential grounding; it is the correct
form for a choice. "We will not build video calling" is not a claim about the world that
evidence could confirm — it is a decision, and what grounds it is *who decided and under
what authority*. IE2.1 already records exactly that on every admission (`author`,
`human_actor_id`, covering `AuthorityRecord`), so the machinery exists.

## 3–5. What needs basis, per kind

`MAY_TERMINATE` = may end a chain. `INTERMEDIATE` = may appear mid-chain but the chain must
continue past it.

| kind | grounding modes | may be basis for others | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `INTENT` | **root**: authoritative only | intermediate (via `SERVES`, never `DERIVED_FROM`) | `I-SEP-1` already forbids `DERIVED_FROM → INTENT` |
| `GOAL` | authoritative or evidential | intermediate | a goal is a choice; evidence may inform it |
| `OUTCOME` | authoritative or evidential | intermediate | |
| `REQUIREMENT` | either | intermediate | a regulation-driven requirement is evidential; an owner-stated one is authoritative |
| `CONSTRAINT` | **determined by facet** (§10) | intermediate | the facet already encodes which mode applies |
| `NON_GOAL` | **authoritative only** | never a basis | an exclusion is a choice; demanding evidence would force fake evidence |
| `PREFERENCE` | **authoritative only** | never a basis | "I prefer dark mode" is self-grounding through authorship |
| `DECISION` | authoritative, **plus its own complete chain** (§5 below) | intermediate only | never terminal |
| `ASSUMPTION` | authoritative (a stated presumption) | **never a basis** | see §12 |
| `CONTRACT` | legacy | legacy | not part of the target ontology; IE2.2 adds no new obligation |

**ProjectDecision is never a magical terminal truth.** A `Constraint` deriving from a
Decision is grounded only if the Decision itself is grounded — authenticated human author,
covering `AuthorityRecord`, and its own `SERVES` path to the root. The graph must answer
*who chose it, under what authority, and why it belongs to this Intent*. IE2.1 records all
three on admission; IE2.2 checks them transitively.

## 4. What may lawfully terminate a chain

The old wording — "terminates in `CLAIM` or `EVIDENCE`, or external `Constraint` +
`AuthorityRecord`" — is wrong in two ways.

**`EVIDENCE` should not be a terminal basis directly.** `SemanticClaim` already carries
`evidence_ids` and `provenance`; a claim *is* the assertion, evidence is what supports it.
Allowing a normative object to derive straight from an `EvidenceItem` bypasses the claim
layer where authority and judgment live.

**A `Claim` is terminal, but only while live.** Required at canonicalization:

- the claim exists in `state.semantic.claims`;
- its `created_by_judgment_id` ∈ `active_judgment_ids(state.semantic)`;
- every id in `claim.evidence_ids` is present in `state.semantic.evidence`.

**Not** required: that the evidence is the newest version. Existing law says a claim citing
superseded evidence is not thereby stale, and IE2.2 does not overturn it. At most a future
slice may surface it as a readiness *signal*; it is not an ungrounding.

So the lawful terminals are: **a live `SemanticClaim`** (evidential mode), or **authenticated
human authorship + covering `AuthorityRecord`** (authoritative mode). Nothing else.

## 6–7. Relevance and the root Intent

- `I-REL-1` — every **active, canonical, in-scope** normative object except the root `INTENT` has a `SERVES` path reaching the project's canonical root `INTENT`.
- `I-REL-2` — every edge on that path stays within the object's scope.

**Open decision (§21-A): exactly one canonical root Intent per project.** Closure today
requires only that *some* canonical `Intent` exists, so several are representable. "Reaches
the root" is meaningless without a unique root. Recommendation: exactly one canonical root
per project; more than one is a readiness blocker rather than an admission refusal, since
history may already contain several.

**Sub-intents are not expressible today** and IE2.2 should not add them: `SERVES` legal
sources exclude `INTENT`, so an Intent cannot serve another Intent. Leaving it closed is the
smaller move; opening it is a separate ontology decision.

## 8–9. Cycles

`SERVES` is transitive, so a cycle would let a set of objects mutually justify their own
relevance and satisfy `I-REL-1` while reaching no root. The same hazard applies to
`DERIVED_FROM`: `A ← B ← A` gives every node a parent and grounds nothing.

**Refused at admission**, not at readiness. When admitting `X` with an edge to `Y`, the
graph already contains `Y`, so walking forward from `Y` and finding `X` is decidable at
write time — and admission is where refusal costs nothing. Deferring to readiness would
allow the cycle to be written and then permanently block delivery with no clean repair.

Both walks are bounded by the number of objects and must carry an explicit visited set, so a
pre-existing historical cycle makes the check terminate and report rather than hang.

## 10. ConstraintFacet proof rules (closing R30)

| facet | what IE2.2 must prove |
|---|---|
| `EVIDENCE_BOUND` | evidential mode: chain terminates in a live `Claim` |
| `PROJECT_BOUNDARY` | authoritative mode: human author + covering `AuthorityRecord` |
| `EXTERNAL_MANDATE` | evidential mode **plus** an external attestation (below) |

**Open decision (§21-B): externality is not derivable from what exists.** `Provenance`
records `source_kind` and `source_ref`; `AuthorityRecord` records `authorized_by`,
`subject_id`, `rationale` and scope. Nothing distinguishes a regulation from an internal
design document — `DOCUMENT` provenance fits both, and `SYSTEM`/`CODE`/`TEST`/`RUNTIME` are
plainly not external mandates. No deterministic rule over current structures can tell them
apart, and inferring it from `source_ref` text would be the keyword heuristic §18 forbids.

Two honest options:

- **(B1, recommended) Externality is an attested human judgement, not an inferred property.** A human with covering authority declares the mandate external, and the declaration is the existing `AuthorityRecord` for that Constraint, with the facet on the object recording what was attested. Deterministic — the record exists or it does not — and it matches how Foundry treats authority everywhere else: humans decide, the system checks and records.
- **(B2) Add structure** — e.g. an origin marker on `AuthorityRecord` distinguishing internal from external mandating bodies. Strictly more expressive, but it is new ontology and a bigger change than IE2.2 should make unasked.

Under B1, `EXTERNAL_MANDATE` requires: evidential basis, a covering `AuthorityRecord`, and
the already-enforced rule that its provenance is not `HUMAN`. IE2.2 would then claim exactly
what it proves — *a human with authority attested this is external* — and not that the system
verified externality.

## 11. Historical compatibility

The load-bearing distinction:

```
REPLAY VALIDITY       can this stream be reconstructed?        must never change
DELIVERY VALIDITY     is current state fit to hand off?        may tighten over time
```

IE2.2 adds **no** parse-time, reducer-time or replay-time validation. Old objects lacking
`SERVES`, complete chains or facet proof replay exactly as they do today; they may simply be
reported unready. Tightening what counts as *ready* is legitimate; tightening what counts as
*reconstructable* would break the property the model certifications rest on.

## 12. Assumption, Preference, NonGoal

**`Assumption` is epistemic and must never ground canonical truth.** It is in the frozen
`INTENT_BEARING_SEMANTIC_KINDS`, and the brief is right that a frozen vocabulary must not
dictate ontology: IE2.2 keeps it admissible but bars it from every basis chain (`I-BASIS-3`).
"We assume peak traffic is under 10k rps" is a presumption, not a fact, and a canonical
Requirement resting on it would be canonical by assumption. The correct edge is
`Assumption ──AFFECTS──▶ Requirement`, which IE2.1's matrix already permits and which IE2.3
will traverse when an assumption is falsified. IE2.2 establishes the dependency; it does not
act on it.

**`Preference` and `NonGoal` are authoritative-only.** Requiring evidence for "I prefer dark
mode" or "we will not build video calling" would force fabricated evidence into normative
human choices — the precise failure §16 of the brief warns against. They need relevance
(`SERVES`) and authoritative grounding, nothing more.

## 13. Certified Slice-1 compatibility

**No conflict, and not by accommodation — by the shape of the rule.** The certified
Requirement is `PROPOSED`, so canonical basis and relevance rules do not reach it. It carries
no `SERVES` edge, which under a rule applying to all objects would have failed; under a
CANONICAL-gated rule it is simply out of scope.

**Basis validation reads object-local `DERIVED_FROM` relations, never `DerivationEdge`.** The
two parent-id conventions still differ — Slice-1 edges point at the claim's
`created_by_judgment_id`, the IE2 seam at the `DERIVED_FROM` target — so any rule reading
edges would have to reconcile them. Reading relations avoids that entirely and leaves
normalization to IE2.3, where it belongs. Nothing is backfilled or rewritten.

## 14–15. Enforcement layer for every rule

| rule | layer | why there |
|---|---|---|
| relation legality, `I-SEP-1` | admission (IE2.1, done) | purely local to one object |
| basis/relevance cycle introduction | **admission** | decidable at write time; refusing costs nothing |
| basis completeness for `CANONICAL` | **admission, gated on authority** | the graph must already support a canonical claim at the moment it is made |
| relevance path for `CANONICAL` | **admission, gated on authority** | same |
| facet proof (§10) | **admission, gated on authority** | it is a property of the object being canonicalized |
| exactly one canonical root Intent | **closure/readiness** | history may hold several; refusing admission cannot repair that |
| orphaned/ungrounded historical canonical objects | **closure/readiness** | they exist; they must be reported, not retroactively refused |

Non-canonical objects are deliberately unconstrained: the graph is built incrementally, and
a Requirement proposed before its Goal exists is normal work, not an error.

## 18–19. RED-first tests and controls

RED must show, before implementation: a canonical object with no basis is admitted; one with
no relevance path is admitted; a two-node `SERVES` cycle is admitted; a two-node
`DERIVED_FROM` cycle satisfies "every node has a parent"; a canonical object grounded in a
dead claim is admitted; an `Assumption` terminates a chain.

Controls must kill at least: evidential grounding accepted for `NonGoal`/`Preference`
(forcing fake evidence); authoritative grounding accepted for `EVIDENCE_BOUND`; `Assumption`
admitted as terminal basis; dead-claim basis accepted; cycle detection without a visited set
(hangs); `DerivationEdge` used for basis instead of relations; canonical rules applied to
`PROPOSED` objects (**which would break the certified path** — the sharpest control here);
replay-time validation introduced; superseded *evidence* treated as ungrounding, contradicting
accepted law.

## 20. Proposed slices

IE2.2 should split — cycles are a different kind of rule from completeness, and landing both
at once would mix a local write-time refusal with graph-global traversal:

- **IE2.2a — cycle prevention.** `SERVES` and `DERIVED_FROM` cycle refusal at admission, with visited sets. Small, local, no canonical gating.
- **IE2.2b — grounding modes and basis completeness.** The two-mode law, terminal rules, `Assumption` exclusion, canonical gating.
- **IE2.2c — relevance completeness and root Intent.** `I-REL-1`/`I-REL-2`, plus the root-uniqueness readiness blocker.
- **IE2.2d — facet proof.** Closes R30 once §21-B is decided.

## 21. Decisions required before implementation

- **A. Root Intent cardinality.** Exactly one canonical root per project? (Recommended; enforced at readiness, not admission.) Sub-intents stay unexpressible unless you want them opened.
- **B. Externality.** B1 (attested by a human with authority — recommended) or B2 (new structure on `AuthorityRecord`)? IE2.2d cannot proceed without this, and no deterministic rule over today's structures can decide externality on its own.
- **C. Requirement grounding.** I propose `REQUIREMENT` accepts either mode. The stricter alternative — requirements must always be evidential — would forbid an owner simply stating an obligation, which seems wrong but is your call.
- **D. Historical canonical objects that fail the new rules.** Report as readiness blockers (recommended), or leave entirely unjudged until superseded?
