## Repaired-harness rerun

After review found the scorers permissive about missing execution evidence, the exam was
repaired and the full 15-call certification was rerun once against this same frozen
contestant. **That rerun did not complete.** Fourteen calls succeeded; Case E attempt 2
raised `ModelProviderError` from `model_runtime/runtime.py`, and the completeness assertion
then refused to emit a 14-call baseline.

The failure is transport, not semantic. It is classified by construction rather than by
inspection: `grpc.RpcError` is the only exception the xAI adapter propagates unconverted, and
every other adapter fault becomes `ModelProtocolError` or `ModelRequestError` — both
`ModelRuntimeError`, which the runtime re-raises unwrapped. A `ModelProviderError` can
therefore only wrap a raw transport fault. The model never returned a scoreable answer on
that attempt, so nothing about its competence was measured or impugned.

**Verdict: `MR4 CERTIFICATION RUN INCOMPLETE: TRANSPORT FAILURE`.** Per the standing rule, no
retry was attempted. No measurement from the partial run was spliced into this record: the
measured baseline below is still the original complete run, and the Case A ledger artifact was
restored to its committed state rather than left half-refreshed. A completing rerun under the
repaired harness remains outstanding and requires explicit authorisation.

The repaired gate does not change the verdict this document records, and that is checkable
rather than asserted: every one of the original 15 calls carried complete provider execution
evidence — provider, model, tokens, cost, latency and finish reason all present, verifiable in
`tests/certification/_last_run_measurements.json`. The permissive branch never excused a
single call. It was a latent hole in the harness, not a load-bearing one in the result.

## Replay without a provider

The event stream from a passing Case A run is preserved at
`tests/certification/_live_case_a_ledger.json`. With a provider bomb armed — any model
construction or synthesizer call fails the test — that ledger replays identically three
ways (direct, JSON-reparsed through `parse_event`, and a second fresh replay), reproducing
`IntentState`, the synthesis decisions, the Requirement, the author fingerprint, authority,
relations and provenance. Replay reads the decision; it never recreates it.

## Authority containment

The model-authored Requirement is `PROPOSED` / `LOW`. Task certification granted
permission to **compute**, not semantic authority: the Requirement does not enter
`contract.obligation_ids`, appears only in `proposed_intent_object_ids`, and closure and
package behaviour are exactly what they already are for any `PROPOSED LOW` Requirement.
Certifying the model changed none of that, and a later CANONICAL promotion would require
human authority through the existing governance path.

## Scoring method

Deterministic only. Structural expectations plus Foundry's own validators. Grok was never
asked whether it answered correctly, its confidence was never a pass criterion, and no
second model graded it. The scorers live in `tests/certification/_intent_synthesis_exam.py`
and are themselves falsified offline by 35 negative controls in
`test_intent_synthesis_exam_harness.py`, which feed them the exact wrong answers the exam
claims to detect.

Absence of evidence is one of those wrong answers. A live attempt that produced no provider
execution evidence fails the exam rather than passing unverified: the gate rejects a missing
measurement before it checks provider, model, task or tier, and no substitute evidence is
synthesised. The certification therefore rests on 15 measured calls, not on 15 calls that
merely failed to contradict it.
