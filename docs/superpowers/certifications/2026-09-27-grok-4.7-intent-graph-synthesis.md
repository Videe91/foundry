## Failure classification

1. **Witness over-application: a contract/prompt ambiguity, exposed by the model.** In 19 of
   23 answered attempts Grok listed the root Intent and the Goal it `SERVES` in
   `unchanged_object_refs`, and twice (G1, G3) the conflicting NonGoal. It reads R111 as
   "existing objects that stay unchanged, or that my graph relies on", not "an existing object
   that already expresses *this claim's* meaning". The prompt does not scope a witness to the
   meaning being synthesized, and no deterministic law rejects an extra witness. This caused
   every failure in A, B, D, E and H, and G1/G3.
2. **Real change was minted as new intent: a model/task failure (C, 0/3).** Grok never used
   `REPLACES_STALE` for the stale `REQ-old`. It minted a new fourteen-day Requirement and raised
   a gap naming `REQ-old`, leaving the stale object in place.
3. **Replacing and referencing the same target (F3): a prompt gap plus a model error.** Grok
   replaced `REQ-stale` and also anchored a gap to it. Foundry refused it correctly with
   `RETIRING_TARGET_REFERENCED`. The prompt does not say that a retired target may not also be
   referenced.
4. **B2 was an INCOMPLETE transport failure.** It is not a verdict about the model.

Secondary observations, not scored: A1 and A2 chose `CONSTRAINT` rather than `REQUIREMENT`;
several clear claims were answered with an extra blocking gap (A2, A3, E1–E3, H1–H3).

Where it mattered, the core semantics were largely right:

- **B:** pure NO_CHANGE naming `REQ-existing`, and no duplicate.
- **H:** a new payment Requirement plus the `REQ-existing` witness, and no duplicate window.
- **D:** refused to invent the missing choice.
- **E:** never referenced the hidden object.
- **G:** raised the CONTRADICTION gap every time.

## Scorer caveat, stated rather than corrected

The C and F scorers do not apply the "no extra witness" rule that A, B, D, E, G and H apply. F1
and F2 passed while witnessing `INTENT-payments` and `GOAL-refunds`; under the A/B/D/E/G/H
standard they would have failed. Re-scoring after seeing results is not permitted, so the
recorded verdicts stand. The inconsistency cannot change the outcome: the verdict is NOT
CERTIFIED either way.

## Open questions for the architect (not decided here)

1. Should a witness be scoped to the meaning being synthesized, and should the prompt say so
   (e.g. "never list an object merely because your new nodes SERVE or reference it")? Should a
   deterministic law refuse a witness that is also the target of a relation in the same result,
   or one that expresses no basis claim?
2. Should the prompt state that a node being replaced may not also be referenced elsewhere in
   the same result?
3. Should the prompt make REPLACES_STALE the expected response when a shown stale object's
   basis has been corrected, rather than a restraint-only condition?
